## SUBJECT 11 — Onboarding Without the Overwhelm — Your First Week
**Category:** Foundational

---

### PART A — 45-Second Short

The first week with a new AI agent is the hardest.
Not because it's complicated — because it's unfamiliar.

Here's what actually helps.

Pick one task. Just one.
Use the agent for that task every day for a week.
Don't try to do everything at once.

By day seven, it won't feel new anymore.
It'll feel like a tool you actually know how to use.

That's the whole onboarding strategy.
One task. One week. Then expand.

If you want a guided starting point for the agent you've chosen —
AskStella has one ready for you.

---

### PART B — 3m15 Mid-Form

**Beat 1 — Why the First Week Matters**

Most people who give up on AI agents
give up in the first week.

Not because the tool is bad.
Because the first week feels awkward and they take that as a sign
that it's not for them.

It's not a sign of anything except newness.

Every tool feels awkward at first.
Your first week driving a car was not smooth.
Your first week at a new job was not comfortable.
That's not failure — that's the learning curve doing its job.

The difference with AI agents is that most people
try to do too much too fast.
They want to overhaul their entire workflow on day one.
And when it doesn't click immediately, they walk away.

There's a better way.

---

**Beat 2 — The One-Task Method**

Here's the approach that actually works.

Pick one task. The most obvious one.
The thing you most want help with.
Writing, summarising, planning — whatever it is.

Use the agent for that one task every day for a week.
Nothing else. Just that.

By day three, you'll start to understand how it thinks.
By day five, you'll know how to phrase things to get better results.
By day seven, it won't feel new anymore.

Then — and only then — add a second task.

This isn't slow. This is how you actually build a habit
instead of a frustrating experiment.

The people who get the most out of AI agents
are not the ones who tried everything at once.
They're the ones who started narrow and went deep.

---

**Beat 3 — What Good Looks Like at Day Seven**

By the end of your first week, you should feel three things.

Comfortable — the interface isn't strange anymore.
Curious — you're starting to wonder what else it can do.
Confident — you've gotten at least one genuinely good result.

If you feel those three things, you're exactly where you should be.

If you don't — that's worth looking at.
Maybe the task wasn't the right starting point.
Maybe the agent isn't the right fit.
Both of those are fixable.

AskStella can help you find the right starting point
for whichever agent you've chosen.
So your first week is a foundation — not a false start.

Just ask AskStella.

## SUBJECT 12 — How to Ask an Agent Good Questions
**Category:** Foundational

---

### PART A — 45-Second Short

The single biggest factor in how well an AI agent performs?
How you ask.

Vague question — vague answer.
Specific question — specific answer.

Here's the shift that changes everything.

Instead of: "help me write an email."
Try: "write a polite follow-up email to a client who hasn't responded in two weeks."

Same task. Completely different result.

The more context you give, the better the output.
You don't need to learn a new language.
You just need to be specific.

AskStella shows you how to ask better questions
for every agent we recommend.

---

### PART B — 3m15 Mid-Form

**Beat 1 — The Skill Nobody Teaches**

There's a skill at the centre of every good AI interaction.

It's not technical. It doesn't require any background knowledge.
But it makes the difference between an agent that feels useless
and one that feels like it can read your mind.

The skill is asking good questions.

Or more precisely — giving good instructions.

Most people approach an AI agent the way they'd approach a search engine.
Short. Vague. Keyword-based.

"Write email."
"Summarise this."
"Give me ideas."

And they get short, vague, generic results.
Then they decide the agent isn't very good.

The agent isn't the problem.

---

**Beat 2 — What Good Actually Looks Like**

Here's the principle.

An AI agent performs at the level of the instructions it receives.

The more context you give it, the better it performs.
The more specific you are, the more useful the output.

Let's make that concrete.

Vague: "write me a cover letter."
Better: "write a cover letter for a marketing manager role at a mid-sized tech company. I have eight years of experience in digital marketing and I want to sound confident but not arrogant."

Vague: "summarise this document."
Better: "summarise this document in three bullet points. Focus on the key decisions made, not the background context."

Vague: "give me ideas for my business."
Better: "I run a small bakery in a residential neighbourhood. Give me five low-cost marketing ideas that don't require social media."

Same agent. Completely different results.

The difference is specificity.
Role, context, tone, format, constraints.
The more of those you include, the closer the output is to what you actually need.

---

**Beat 3 — The One Habit That Changes Everything**

You don't need to master this overnight.

Start with one habit.

Before you type your request — add one sentence of context.
Who you are, what you're trying to achieve, or what the output should look like.

That one sentence will improve your results immediately.

And over time, as you use the agent more,
you'll naturally get better at this.
You'll learn what it responds well to.
You'll develop your own shorthand.

It becomes second nature.

AskStella includes starter prompts for every agent we recommend —
so you don't have to figure out the right way to ask from scratch.

Just ask AskStella.

## SUBJECT 13 — When an Agent Isn't Working For You
**Category:** Foundational

---

### PART A — 45-Second Short

Not every AI agent is going to be the right fit.
And that's completely normal.

If you've given it a fair go — a week, a real task, genuine effort —
and it's still not clicking, that's information.

It might be the wrong tool for your task.
It might be the wrong category entirely.
Or it might just need a different approach.

Before you walk away from AI altogether —
figure out which of those three it is.

Quitting the wrong tool is smart.
Quitting the right tool too early is a missed opportunity.

AskStella can help you work out the difference.

---

### PART B — 3m15 Mid-Form

**Beat 1 — Permission to Quit**

Here's something worth saying clearly.

You are allowed to stop using an AI agent that isn't working for you.

You don't owe it more time.
You don't have to keep pushing through frustration.
If it's not delivering value after a genuine effort — move on.

But — and this matters —
before you move on, it's worth understanding why it didn't work.

Because "this agent isn't right for me"
and "AI agents aren't right for me"
are two very different conclusions.

One of them closes a door that might genuinely help you.
The other just redirects you to a better fit.

---

**Beat 2 — The Three Reasons It's Not Working**

When an agent isn't delivering, it's usually one of three things.

The first is task mismatch.
The agent is good — just not for what you're asking it to do.
A foundation agent asked to do specialist work will underperform.
A creative writing agent asked to handle legal documents will struggle.
The fix: find the agent built for your specific task.

The second is expectation mismatch.
You expected magic. You got a capable tool.
The agent is performing fine — but not at the level you imagined.
The fix: recalibrate. Work with what it actually does well.

The third is approach mismatch.
The agent is right. The task is right.
But the way you're asking isn't getting the results you need.
The fix: try being more specific. Give it more conOften this alone turns a frustrating experience into a good one.

---

**Beat 3 — How to Decide What's Next**

Before you make any decision, ask yourself three questions.

Did I give it enough time? At least a week of real use.
Did I give it the right task? One it's actually built for.
Did I ask it well? With enough context and specificity.

If the answer to all three is yes — and it's still not working —
then it's genuinely not the right fit. Move on.

If the answer to any of them is no —
try adjusting that one thing before you decide.

AskStella can help you figure out which of these it is
and point you toward a better match if you need one.

Just ask AskStella.

## SUBJECT 14 — Using More Than One Agent — Without Losing Your Mind
**Category:** Foundational

---

### PART A — 45-Second Short

At some point, one agent won't cover everything you need.
And that's fine — as long as you're deliberate about it.

The mistake people make is adding tools without a system.
Suddenly they have five agents, none of them used well,
and more complexity than they started with.

The rule: each agent gets one job.
One for writing. One for research. One for organisation.
Clear lanes. No overlap.

Two agents used well beats five used badly every time.

When you're ready to expand your stack —
AskStella can help you build it without the chaos.

---

### PART B — 3m15 Mid-Form

**Beat 1 — The Expansion Temptation**

Once you've had a good experience with one AI agent,
something interesting happens.

You start noticing the gaps.

The things your agent doesn't do well.
The tasks where you wish it was a bit more specialised.
The areas where you've heard another tool is better.

And the temptation is to add another agent.
Then another.
Then another.

Before long you have a collection of tools,
none of them used to their full potential,
and a workflow that's more complicated than the one you started with.

This is the expansion trap.
And it's worth knowing about before you fall into it.

---

**Beat 2 — The One-Job Rule**

Here's the principle that keeps a multi-agent setup sane.

Each agent gets one job.

Not "I'll use this one for most things and that one sometimes."
One clear, defined job.

This one handles my writing.
This one handles my research.
This one handles my scheduling.

When the lanes are clear, there's no confusion about which tool to reach for.
You don't waste time deciding. You just go to the right one.

The second principle: start with two before you go to three.

Get two agents working well together before you add a third.
Make sure the workflow is smooth. Make sure there's no overlap.
Then, if there's a genuine gap — fill it.

Two agents used well is a genuinely powerful setup.
Five agents used badly is just noise.

---

**Beat 3 — Building a Stack That Works for You**

A personal AI stack doesn't have to be complicated.

For most people, two or three agents covers everything.

A foundation agent for general tasks.
A specialist for the one area where you need more depth.
Maybe a third for a very specific recurring need.

That's it.

The goal isn't to have the most tools.
The goal is to have the right tools, used well,
that make your life measurably better.

When you're ready to think about expanding beyond one agent —
AskStella can help you build a stack that makes sense for your life.
No overlap. No chaos. Just the right tools in the right lanes.

Just ask AskStella.
