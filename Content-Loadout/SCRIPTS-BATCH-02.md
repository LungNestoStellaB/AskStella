## SUBJECT 6 — Specialist vs. Foundation Agent — What's the Difference?
**Category:** Foundational

---

### PART A — 45-Second Short

There are two kinds of AI agents.

Foundation agents do a bit of everything.
They write, research, summarise, plan.
Think of them as a very capable all-rounder.

Specialist agents do one thing — and do it exceptionally well.
Legal documents. Code. Design. Language learning.
Built for a specific job.

Most people start with a foundation agent.
Then add a specialist when they find a gap.

Knowing the difference saves you from picking the wrong tool
and wondering why it's not performing.

Not sure which type fits your situation?
AskStella can point you in the right direction.

---

### PART B — 3m15 Mid-Form

**Beat 1 — Two Very Different Tools**

When people talk about AI agents, they usually mean one of two things —
and they don't always realise there's a difference.

The first type is a foundation agent.
It's broad. Flexible. Capable across many tasks.
You can ask it to write an email, summarise a document,
help you plan a trip, or explain a concept.
It won't be the world's best at any one of those things —
but it'll handle all of them competently.

The second type is a specialist agent.
It's built for one lane.
A legal agent that understands contracts.
A coding agent that writes and debugs software.
A language agent that teaches you Spanish.
In its lane, it's exceptional.
Outside its lane, it's not the right tool.

---

**Beat 2 — How to Know Which You Need**

Here's the question that separates them.

Do you have one specific, recurring problem —
or do you have a general need for help across different areas?

If it's one specific problem — a specialist is probably your answer.
If it's general — start with a foundation agent.

Most people, when they're starting out, don't actually know
exactly what they need yet.

And that's fine.

That's exactly why foundation agents exist.
They let you explore. Try things. Figure out where the value is for you.
Then, once you know — you can add a specialist.

Think of it like this.
A foundation agent is your Swiss Army knife.
A specialist is your scalpel.

You don't start surgery with a Swiss Army knife.
But you don't carry a scalpel to a camping trip either.

---

**Beat 3 — The Right Starting Point**

For most people reading this — start with a foundation agent.

Get comfortable. Learn how to ask good questions.
Discover which tasks feel natural and which don't.

Then, when you hit a wall —
when you need something more precise, more powerful, more specific —
that's when you go looking for the specialist.

AskStella maps both types.
So whether you're starting broad or going deep —
you'll know exactly where to look.

Just ask AskStella.

## SUBJECT 7 — What to Look For Before You Commit to an Agent
**Category:** Foundational

---

### PART A — 45-Second Short

Before you commit to any AI agent, check five things.

Does it work on your device?
Is there a free tier to try before you pay?
How steep is the learning curve?
Does it connect to the tools you already use?
And is there real support when you get stuck?

Those five questions will save you from a lot of frustration.

Most people skip this step and end up locked into something
that doesn't quite fit.

Take ten minutes before you start.
AskStella walks you through the checklist for every agent we recommend.

---

### PART B — 3m15 Mid-Form

**Beat 1 — The Commitment Trap**

Here's something that happens more than it should.

Someone hears about an AI agent.
They sign up. They spend an afternoon learning it.
They start building their workflow around it.

Then they discover it doesn't work on their phone.
Or it doesn't connect to the tools they already use.
Or the free tier runs out after a week and the paid plan is more than they want to spend.

And now they're stuck.

Not because the agent is bad.
Because they didn't check the right things before they started.

This is completely avoidable.

---

**Beat 2 — The Five Things to Check**

Before you commit to any agent, run through these five.

One — platform availability.
Does it work on your device? Your phone, your laptop, your browser?
If you need it on the go and it's desktop only, that's a problem.

Two — free tier.
Almost every good agent has one.
What does it include? What are the limits?
Can you actually test it properly before spending money?

Three — learning curve.
Some agents are ready to use in five minutes.
Others take days to configure.
Be honest about how much time you want to invest upfront.

Four — integrations.
Does it connect to the tools you already use?
Email, calendar, documents, notes.
An agent that lives in isolation is less powerful than one that fits your existing life.

Five — support.
When you get stuck — and you will, at least once — where do you go?
Good documentation, an active community, or responsive customer support
is the difference between a frustrating experience and a smooth one.

---

**Beat 3 — The Ten-Minute Check**

None of this takes long.

Ten minutes of research before you start
saves hours of frustration after.

And the good news is — AskStella has already done most of this work for you.

Every agent we recommend comes with a breakdown of exactly these five things.
Platform. Free tier. Learning curve. Integrations. Support.

So you're not going in blind.
You're going in informed.

That's the whole point.

## SUBJECT 8 — Free vs. Paid — What You Actually Get
**Category:** Foundational

---

### PART A — 45-Second Short

Here's the honest truth about free AI agents.

Most of them are genuinely good.
The free tier isn't a trick to get you to pay.
It's a real product — with real limits.

The limits are usually: how much you can use per day,
how fast the responses are,
and whether you get access to the newest features.

For most people starting out, free is more than enough.

Pay when you've outgrown it.
Not before.

AskStella tells you exactly what each agent's free tier includes —
so you know what you're getting before you sign up.

---

### PART B — 3m15 Mid-Form

**Beat 1 — The Fear Around Paying**

One of the most common reasons people hesitate with AI agents
is the fear of accidentally paying for something.

They've been burned before.
A free trial that quietly became a subscription.
A "freemium" product that locked everything useful behind a paywall.

That fear is completely understandable.
And it's worth addressing directly.

Because the reality of AI agent pricing
is actually more straightforward than most people expect.

---

**Beat 2 — What Free Actually Means**

Most major AI agents have a free tier that is genuinely usable.

Not a demo. Not a teaser. A real product.

What you typically get for free:
access to the core features,
a daily or monthly usage limit,
standard response speed,
and the ability to try before you decide anything.

What you typically get when you pay:
higher usage limits,
faster responses,
access to the most advanced models or features,
and sometimes priority support.

For someone just starting out —
the free tier is almost always enough.

You'll know when you've outgrown it.
You'll hit the limit and think — yes, this is worth paying for.
That's the right moment to upgrade.
Not before.

The agents that don't offer a meaningful free tier?
AskStella flags those clearly.
No surprises.

---

**Beat 3 — How to Approach It**

Here's the approach that works.

Start free. Always.
Use it properly for at least two weeks.
Figure out whether it's actually solving your problem.

If it is — and you're hitting limits — then consider paying.
If it's not solving your problem, no amount of paid features will fix that.

The goal is to find the right tool first.
Then invest in it.

AskStella breaks down the free tier for every agent we recommend.
What's included. What the limits are. What you get if you upgrade.

So you can make that decision with full information.

## SUBJECT 9 — Privacy and Your Data — What You Should Know
**Category:** Foundational

---

### PART A — 45-Second Short

Privacy is the question most people want to ask
but feel embarrassed to.

So let's just answer it.

When you use an AI agent, it processes what you type.
Some agents store that data. Some don't.
Some use it to improve their models. Some let you opt out.

None of that is hidden — it's in the privacy policy.
Which, yes, nobody reads.

The short version: don't share anything with an AI agent
that you wouldn't share with a stranger.
Passwords, financial details, sensitive personal information — keep those out.

AskStella flags the privacy stance of every agent we recommend.
So you know before you start.

---

### PART B — 3m15 Mid-Form

**Beat 1 — The Question People Don't Ask**

Privacy is the thing most people worry about
but very few people actually investigate.

They either assume it's fine and don't think about it —
or they assume it's dangerous and avoid AI entirely.

Both of those are the wrong response.

The truth is somewhere in the middle.
And it's worth understanding — not to scare you,
but so you can use these tools confidently
without giving away more than you intend to.

---

**Beat 2 — What Actually Happens to Your Data**

When you type something into an AI agent, that text is processed by a server.

What happens next depends on the agent.

Some agents store your conversations to improve their models.
Some give you the option to turn that off.
Some delete your data after each session.
Some enterprise-grade agents are built specifically for sensitive environments
and have strict data handling policies.

None of this is secret.
It's all in the privacy policy — which, yes, is long and nobody reads it.

But here's the practical summary.

Most consumer AI agents are not appropriate for:
passwords, financial account details, medical records,
legal documents with sensitive information,
or anything you'd consider genuinely private.

They are completely fine for:
writing help, research, planning, learning, creative work,
and the vast majority of everyday tasks.

The rule is straightforward.
If you wouldn't say it to a stranger in a coffee shop —
don't type it into an AI agent.

---

**Beat 3 — How to Stay Comfortable**

Here's what confident, informed AI use looks like.

You know which agent you're using and roughly what their data policy is.
You keep sensitive information out of your prompts.
You use the privacy settings available — most agents have them.
And you don't let privacy concerns stop you from using tools
that are genuinely safe for the tasks you're doing.

AskStella flags the privacy stance of every agent we recommend.
Not a legal summary — a plain-language note on what to know.

So you can make an informed choice
and use these tools without second-guessing yourself.

Just ask AskStella.

## SUBJECT 10 — Security Basics — Protecting Yourself While Using Agents
**Category:** Foundational

---

### PART A — 45-Second Short

Using AI agents safely comes down to a few basics.

Use a strong, unique password for every account.
Turn on two-factor authentication where it's offered.
Don't give an agent more permissions than it needs.
And if something asks for access to your email or files —
read what it's actually asking for before you say yes.

None of this is complicated.
It's the same good habits that protect you anywhere online.

AI agents aren't a special security risk.
But they're not an exception to good practice either.

AskStella covers the security basics for every agent we recommend.
So you start safe and stay safe.

---

### PART B — 3m15 Mid-Form

**Beat 1 — Security Isn't Scary**

Let's get something out of the way.

AI agents are not a unique security threat.

The risks involved in using them are the same risks
that exist anywhere you have an online account.
Weak passwords. Phishing. Oversharing permissions.

The good news is — the same habits that protect you everywhere online
protect you here too.

You don't need to learn anything new.
You just need to apply what you probably already know
to this new category of tool.

---

**Beat 2 — The Four Habits That Matter**

One — strong, unique passwords.

Every AI agent account should have its own password.
Not the one you use for email. Not the one you use for banking.
Its own password, stored in a password manager if you have one.

Two — two-factor authentication.

Most major AI agents offer this.
Turn it on. It takes two minutes and it means
that even if someone gets your password, they can't get in.

Three — permissions awareness.

Some agents ask for access to your email, calendar, or files.
Before you say yes — read what they're asking for.
Does this agent actually need access to my entire inbox?
Or just the document I'm working on?
Grant the minimum needed. Nothing more.

Four — account hygiene.

If you stop using an agent, close the account.
Don't leave dormant accounts sitting around with access to your data.
A quick audit every few months keeps things clean.

---

**Beat 3 — The Right Mindset**

Security isn't about fear.
It's about being deliberate.

You lock your front door not because you're afraid —
but because it's a sensible habit.

Same principle here.

Strong password. Two-factor on. Permissions checked. Dormant accounts closed.

That's it. That's the whole thing.

AskStella notes any specific security considerations
for every agent we recommend —
so you know what to look out for before you start.

Just ask AskStella.
